# Museum planning: principles and working method

Distilled from the Vrindavan and SPN planning work with Paragata Dasi, 20–24 October 2026. These are planning principles, not a live placement register or a description of every currently implemented feature. Current curator decisions, inventory and physical constraints take precedence over the examples below.

## The main idea

Build a legible regional museum out of intact personal groups. Let a visitor discover relationships through neighboring displays, while the curator can tell exactly what belongs together, where it should go, and what still needs checking.

Regions provide orientation. Existing subsections preserve the distinctions between traditions. Reviewed families and direct relationships determine which saints should stay close. Each vitrine needs a coherent main story, but its headline need not exclude every other separately labelled holding.

## 1. Bring the same saint together before optimizing the room

First find relics of the same securely identified saint scattered across shelves or vitrines. Plan the complete display: relic containers, portrait, label and printed biography. Preserve separate source observations and object identities; co-location is not a database merge.

Keep a portrait in place and bring containers to it when that reduces handling and the resulting location fits the approved story. This is a practical tie-breaker, not a rule to preserve a poor location. A frame or box describes housing and does not establish whether there is a portrait.

## 2. Keep close relationships together, with their actual meanings

Direct guru–disciple relationships, close biological or devotional households, and immediate successors are strong reasons for sharing a vitrine. “Family” includes these spiritual relationships, not only relatives. Inspect exact identities and relationship roles before moving a group.

Distinguish these from shared tradition, devotional influence, traditional avatar groupings, documented encounters and suggested curatorial adjacency. They can all enrich a display, but they do not all create a guru–disciple chain. A large lineage does not need to fit in one case: keep its immediate branches together and connect neighboring cases.

An inherited display-family key is a planning lead, not independent historical evidence. Retain contested membership or identity qualifications. A same-name record is not enough to merge people or move all their source observations together.

Examples from our work: teacher–disciple reunions such as Tinkodi–Murari; Devraha–Devra Hansa; and the Meher Baba formation circle. The latter requires labels that preserve the different relationships rather than representing every figure as the same kind of teacher.

## 3. Use regions without flattening traditions or lives

Use the hierarchy **region → named subsection → reviewed display family → saint**, while allowing the physical layout to accommodate several clearly distinguished groups. Region names do not replace subsection identifiers, nested tradition labels or family trees.

Give reviewed direct relationships priority over a geographic default. A saint can have several important life-localities, and a lineage can span regions. Use the life and work of the people in this actual display to choose an anchor, rather than birthplace, collection place, nationality or present-day pilgrimage location alone.

Where identity research remains incomplete, an explicit source locality can support provisional regional planning if the user has authorized that fallback. It does not verify the identity, biographical significance of that locality or a lineage affiliation.

For example, the Mother, Dilip Kumar Roy and Indira Devi offered a South–West connection. We could identify that connection in a vitrine’s focus while retaining an existing regional parent; every bridge does not need a new region. The Jaiguru group similarly illustrated keeping an immediate lineage together despite wider Sri Vaishnava connections.

## 4. Make regional neighborhoods compact

Begin with one workable, compact baseline. Refine it before generating many variations; otherwise the same weaknesses multiply across alternatives.

Treat the floorplan as a graph of visitor-visible connections. Distinguish consecutive cases on one face, endcaps, facing rows, back-to-back faces, passages and different rooms. Numerical proximity alone is not adjacency. In SPN, Paragata explicitly preferred continuous adjacent displays over an across-aisle connection where the space allowed it.

Avoid isolated regional fragments unless a specific relationship, fixed object or physical constraint justifies them. A neighboring region can support a bridge at the boundary without interrupting the whole neighborhood.

## 5. Use connections to build transitions visitors can follow

For each important cross-region connection, decide which end of each display should face its neighbor. Explain the named relationship beside the placement recommendation. This turns research into an arrangement the curator can use without doing further research.

The SPN Mirabai/Vraj → Ravidas and Vraj Ramanandis → Tulsidas/Rama → Kabir/Kashi sequence is an example of distributing a story along neighboring cases rather than crowding every participant into one vitrine. It mixes personal/traditional links and curatorial transitions; labels must preserve that distinction.

Other useful opportunities included Gaudiya–Puri, Andhra–western Datta, and Nath–Varkari transitions. Use a contextual ancestor in a tree when it helps explain the connection, but distinguish that figure from confirmed museum holdings.

## 6. Give prominence and readability their own roles

Reserve prominent viewing positions for recognizable anchors, while keeping room for their close companions. Do not fragment the museum simply to put every famous saint at the entrance. Once groups are stable, compare different placements of whole groups; a rotation can reduce moves without changing the story.

Within a case, give the principal saint the best visible position, then Secondary saints and close families in the remaining upper or comfortable reading positions. Keep each printed biography beside the correct relic. A lesser-known teacher can remain beside a more famous disciple; proximity and tier are different decisions.

Paragata’s project tier rubric recognizes public prominence within a region or tradition, not only international fame. Direct personal affiliation to an independently Primary figure and a substantial credible public biography can support at least Secondary. Direct discussion by Paramahamsa Vishwananda is a review lead, not automatic Primary status. Public prominence also applies to included historical figures. Do not cascade tier promotions indefinitely through a broad family graph.

For the Vrindavan printed-bio work, Paragata authorized at least Secondary for saints with printed bios, while retaining Primary anchors. That is a dated project decision, not a universal rule for every museum or a reason to promote the subject of an accompanying bio as if their own relic were present. Bhagat Puran Singh’s bio accompanying Piara Singh’s relic illustrated why the distinction matters.

## 7. Plan for physical objects, not just names or counts

Record fixed housing, full-height objects, mounting needs, available and future cases, and relocation dependencies before allocating ordinary space. A whole-saint assignment must not move a fixed stick or other immovable component out of its protected housing; use a cross-reference when necessary.

Use museum-specific comparisons. Vrindavan’s source packaging and portrait evidence informed handling and display load. SPN’s number of source observations sharing a shelf supplied a rough occupancy comparison for irregular relic sizes. Neither measure establishes dimensional fit. Quantity text, component counts, source observations and unique saints are different units.

Count unknown footprints separately rather than treating them as zero-size. A sparse recorded case is not proof of an empty case. Reserve space for the family’s companions before presenting an anchor’s destination as settled.

The SPN five-share comparison reference, 1.5-share lower-review allowance, provisional numbers and particular protected cases are dated planning settings. Re-read the current layout and constraints; do not make them universal capacity constants.

## 8. Let unresolved holdings share space without acquiring a false story

Avoid concentrating every uncertain entry in a few overloaded “Needs review” cases. Where authorized, distribute intact review cohorts into separate lower displays below coherent main regional families.

Use an explicit **Identity under review** label and retain the source IDs and uncertainty. The host vitrine’s region, tradition and family do not become attributes of those entries. Review presence must be record-based: an entry with no known footprint or no working placement still needs to be visible.

After redistribution, revisit the freed upper space. It can house a neighboring family and relieve pressure elsewhere, rather than becoming an unexplained gap. In the late SPN proposal this created main displays for Devraha at 12, Kerala/southern ascetic contexts at 15 and Karnataka/Hubli–Mugalkhod at 72, with review entries below. These are examples, not future default placements.

“Potential flexible space” is a comparison cue, not a confirmed vacancy or permission to add an unrelated group. Both flexible-space and unresolved-holdings indicators can appear on the same case. Exclude fixed, reserved, full-height and dependency-blocked spaces from generic spare-space claims.

## 9. Audit every tier and every source observation

After each reallocation, check the full inventory, including newly assigned Tertiary and source-only entries. Check exact endpoints, their actual tiers and their proposed cases. Protecting a Primary teacher while overlooking a newly allocated Tertiary disciple still splits the family.

Verify separately:

- Every source observation is accounted for once; repeated observations are not assumed to be duplicate objects.
- A saint’s scattered movable holdings have one coherent destination, with explicit fixed-object exceptions.
- Reviewed immediate relationships stay together or have a stated practical exception; held identities stay held.
- Family and section identities survive regional regrouping; display adjacency creates no new historical edges.
- The whole-case load includes regional holdings, review infill, unknown footprints and protected objects in their proper units.
- The map, case details, family trees, exports and guide agree on proposed destinations and meanings.

Do not describe a canonical-edge check as exhaustive historical verification of every researched relationship. Record which relationship set, source snapshot and unit the audit covered.

## 10. Make the moves executable in the prescribed order

For an implementation guide, start from the curator's latest confirmed locations. If only source-recorded locations are available, label them as unverified and require an on-site check before the affected move. Give exact origins, destinations and the saints who should end up beside one another. Put the relevant family tree beside that recommendation, with context before the action where context changes the decision.

Simulate the moves in order. After each move, update the working location and available-space ledger. Do not use an original location after an earlier step has changed it, allocate a vacated space twice, or assume an exchange is balanced from equal record counts. State any staging or on-site fit dependency at the step it affects.

Keep the strongest useful groupings even when that takes more movement. The Vrindavan work showed why an apparently easy exchange could wrongly split Namdev–Janabai–Tukaram or leave Motilal away from Kriya. Compare whole-case permutations and actual movable assemblies before declaring a lower-move arrangement better.

## 11. Design review for an honest “I don’t know”

A curator questionnaire should show the exact portrait, label or source entry that needs checking, the question, and what each answer would change. Include an unknown outcome. A curator’s inability to identify someone is not a negative identification or permission to choose the most famous namesake.

Identity uncertainty need not prevent every provisional physical arrangement, but a temporary labelled location must not silently clear the identity hold or create a website relationship. New questions should enter the same maintained queue rather than being scattered across chat and static documents.

## 12. Keep the curator’s reading burden low

Show the few facts needed for the current placement decision: what moves, where it goes, who belongs beside it, and any immediate fit dependency. Put relationship context and the corresponding tree together. Prefer an exact recommendation to an unexplained choice of several vitrines.

Omit generic principles already embodied in the plan, repeated cautions, statements that an action is unnecessary, and speculative connections. Preserve meaningful uncertainty, fixed-object restrictions, current-versus-proposed location, and save/recovery consequences. A short mobile move guide and a fuller optional research view serve different needs.

## Reusable workflow

1. Establish the museum, source/current-state snapshot, curator decisions and physical constraints.
2. Reconcile identity and repeated observations; identify scattered holdings of the same saint.
3. Review tiers separately from the typed relationship and display-family map.
4. Build compact regional neighborhoods, retaining subsections and immediate groups.
5. Place anchors and their companions, then all remaining holdings; inspect pressure across neighboring cases.
6. Label unresolved lower displays and reassess freed main-display space when that policy is authorized.
7. Audit the entire inventory and relationship set; compare movement cost only among coherent, feasible layouts.
8. Prepare the exact ordered guide and proposal-view metadata; validate against fresh records before loading.

Do not freeze this as a one-way waterfall. A fixed object, clarified identity or useful connection can change the regional layout and require another pass.
